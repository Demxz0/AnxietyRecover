Anxiety Recover
By Dema Aljundi
Game Overview
Game Title: Anxiety Recover.
Genre:  Psychological simulation / Puzzle / Narrative. 
Platform(s):  On Ujleek website exclusively , on all browsers.
Target Audience: Generation Z.
Game Description: Anxiety Recover game will help generation Z to learn how to deal with anxiety 
with CBT , medications and exposure through challenges , and calming techniques.
Perspective: First Person.
Game Systems
Anxiety System
The core system of the game is the Anxiety Meter, represented by a UI bar.
Anxiety Levels
The system contains four stages:
1.  Calm
2.  Mild Anxiety
3.  High Anxiety
4.  Panic
When the anxiety meter reaches its maximum value, a panic attack may occur.
However, panic attacks can also occur randomly, reflecting the unpredictable nature of real 
panic attacks.
Anxiety Triggers

The anxiety meter increases due to several factors:
darkness
time pressure
disturbing sounds
staying alone in a room for too long
environmental challenges
random spikes
Reducing Anxiety
Anxiety does not decrease automatically.
It can only decrease through specific player actions:
performing the breathing exercise
solving challenges
interacting with calming elements
Panic Attack System
When a panic attack begins:
the camera shakes
the player’s field of vision narrows
breathing sounds become loud
heartbeat becomes rapid
visual distortions appear on the screen
If the player fails to calm down:
the screen fades to black
the character loses consciousness
the player wakes up in the same location
This avoids a traditional Game Over, reinforcing the idea that panic attacks are not dangerous.
Breathing System
The main calming mechanic is a Breathing Rhythm Mini-Game.
During a panic attack, a circle appears on screen.
Player interaction:
Circle expanding → Press and hold (inhale)
Circle shrinking → Release (exhale)
Following the rhythm for about one minute successfully ends the panic attack and reduces 
anxiety.

Medication System
The player has access to a limited calming medication.
Effects:
instantly stops a panic attack
Penalty:
temporarily lowers the anxiety threshold
future panic attacks occur faster
This encourages the player to rely more on breathing techniques instead of medication.
Gameplay Mechanics
Player Movement
The player can:
walk
run
explore rooms
Interaction Mechanics
Players can interact with the environment by:
interacting with objects
inspecting items
opening drawers and cabinets
reading written notes
These interactions support exploration and narrative discovery.
Environmental Interaction
Many interactions are contextual:
activating lights
collecting small environmental pieces
manipulating objects
These interactions are used primarily to progress through the game's situations.
Game Loop

The gameplay loop follows a repeating psychological cycle.
1. Exploration
The player explores the house and interacts with the environment.
2. Escalation
Environmental triggers gradually increase the anxiety meter.
3. Confrontation
The player encounters one of the psychological challenges.
Examples:
illusion room
looping room
dependency challenge
4. Resolution
The player manages anxiety through:
solving the situation
performing breathing exercises
Afterward, anxiety decreases and the player continues exploring.
World Design
The entire game takes place inside one house.
The layout is consistent, the atmosphere changes based on the player's anxiety level.
Environmental Behavior
Rooms themselves do not physically change, but the player's perception changes through:
lighting changes
sound intensity
camera effects
visual distortions
Key Rooms
Illusion Room
A dark room where the player hears voices of people.

Possible actions:
hide in another room (incorrect)
search for the missing piece of the light switch (correct)
Turning on the light reveals that no one is actually present.
Loop Room
A room where every door leads back to the same room.
Trying to escape repeatedly increases anxiety.
The correct solution is to stop trying to escape and wait calmly.
After a short time, the player can exit.
Dependency Situation
The player initially relies on something that helps solve a challenge.
Later, this assistance disappears.
The player must quickly adapt and find another solution before anxiety rises too high.
Art Style
The game uses a low-poly visual style.
Visual Direction
The aesthetic is designed to feel:
symbolic
dreamlike
psychological
The environment is not intended to be realistic horror, but rather a visual representation of 
mental states.
Color Language
Color plays an important symbolic role.
Orange lighting
represents anxiety and tension
Blue lighting
represents calmness and emotional balance

As the player learns to manage anxiety, the house lighting gradually shifts from orange to 
blue.
UI / UX Design
Anxiety Meter
Displayed as a bar on the screen indicating the current anxiety level.
Intrusive Thought Visualization
Intrusive thoughts appear as:
particle effects on the screen
partially blocking the player's vision
These particles represent overwhelming thoughts.
Guidance System
The game includes guidance by a text that:
explains mechanics
tells the player what to do
And a voice explanation about anxiety, helping the player understand the situation.
Audio Design
Music
The game includes background music that changes depending on anxiety level.
calm music at low anxiety
tense music at high anxiety
Sound Design
Environmental sounds contribute to tension:
distant voices
unsettling ambient noises
breathing sounds
heartbeat sounds
Voice Design

Voices heard in the game are understandable words, representing intrusive thoughts.
A voice explans facts about anxiety in order to learn.
Narrative Design
The player character is anonymous, representing the player rather than a specific individual.
This allows the experience to feel universal.
Environmental Storytelling
The game includes written notes scattered around the house.
These notes provide insight into:
anxiety
coping mechanisms
personal reflections and memories
