Game Design Document (GDD)
Anxiety Recover
By Dema Aljundi

Game Overview
Game Title: Anxiety Recover.
Genre: Psychological simulation / Puzzle / Narrative.
Platform(s): On Ujleek website exclusively, on all browsers (WebGL).
Target Audience: Generation Z.
Game Description: Anxiety Recover game will help generation Z to learn how to deal with anxiety with CBT, medications and exposure through challenges, and calming techniques.
Perspective: First Person.

Game Systems

Anxiety System
The core system of the game is the Anxiety Meter, represented by a UI bar.

Anxiety Levels
The system contains four stages:
1. Calm
2. Mild Anxiety
3. High Anxiety
4. Panic

When the anxiety meter reaches its maximum value, a panic attack may occur. However, panic attacks can also occur randomly, reflecting the unpredictable nature of real panic attacks.

Anxiety Triggers
The anxiety meter increases due to several factors:
- darkness
- time pressure
- disturbing sounds
- staying alone in a room for too long
- environmental challenges
- random spikes

Reducing Anxiety
Anxiety does not decrease automatically. It can only decrease through specific player actions:
- performing the breathing exercise
- solving challenges
- interacting with calming elements

Panic Attack System
When a panic attack begins:
- the camera shakes
- the player’s field of vision narrows (FOV adjustments)
- breathing sounds become loud
- heartbeat becomes rapid
- visual distortions appear on the screen

If the player fails to calm down:
- the screen fades to black
- the character loses consciousness
- the player wakes up in the same location (featuring a "getting up" sequence where the camera rises from floor level to standing height and faces forward)
This avoids a traditional Game Over, reinforcing the idea that panic attacks are not dangerous.

Breathing System
The main calming mechanic is a simple hold-to-breathe system.
During a panic attack or high anxiety, the player can perform a breathing exercise.
- The player holds a designated input to breathe.
- The system provides visual breathing feedback through particle effects.
- Successfully holding and completing the breathing exercise reduces anxiety and reverses environmental visual distortions.

Medication System
The player has access to a limited calming medication.
Effects:
- instantly stops a panic attack
Penalty:
- temporarily lowers the anxiety threshold
- future panic attacks occur faster
This encourages the player to rely more on breathing techniques instead of medication.

Gameplay Mechanics

Player Movement
The player can:
- walk
- run
- explore rooms

Interaction Mechanics
Players interact with the environment using a robust camera-raycast interaction system. Actions include:
- interacting with objects (e.g., interactive kitchen cookie, phone-shaking animations)
- inspecting items
- opening drawers and cabinets
- reading written notes
- using modular puzzle scripts to interact with combo locks or collect items with visual feedback (objects disappearing upon collection).

These interactions support exploration and narrative discovery.

Game Loop
The gameplay loop follows a repeating psychological cycle.
1. Exploration
The player explores the house and interacts with the environment.
2. Escalation
Environmental triggers gradually increase the anxiety meter.
3. Confrontation
The player encounters one of the psychological challenges.
Examples:
- Illusion room (dark bedroom)
- Loop room (kitchen agoraphobia)
- Dependency challenge (office)
4. Resolution
The player manages anxiety through:
- solving the situation
- performing breathing exercises
Afterward, anxiety decreases and the player continues exploring.

World Design
The entire game takes place inside one house. The layout is consistent, but the atmosphere changes based on the player's anxiety level.

Environmental Behavior
Rooms themselves do not physically change geometry, but the player's perception changes through:
- lighting changes
- sound intensity
- camera effects (FOV squeezing, lens distortion, chromatic aberration, vignetting)
- atmospheric particle effects (anxiety-driven environmental dust, relief-based effects)

Key Rooms

Illusion Room (Bedroom)
A dark bedroom where the player hears voices of people.
Possible actions:
- hide in another room (incorrect)
- search for the missing piece of the light switch (correct)
Turning on the light reveals that no one is actually present.

Loop Room (Kitchen)
An agoraphobia-driven room where the player experiences spatial distortion.
- The room visually simulates an "expanding" effect using camera-based post-processing (FOV squeezing, lens distortion) rather than physical geometry changes.
- The anxiety-inducing atmosphere slows the player's movement speed.
- The correct solution is to use the breathing mechanic. Completing breathing exercises is the only way to reverse the visual distortions and restore normal gameplay speed.

Dependency Situation (Office)
An office puzzle scenario where the player must find and interact with objects like a combo lock and collect items like keys and switch pieces.
- The player initially relies on something that helps solve a challenge.
- Later, this assistance disappears.
- The player must quickly adapt, interact with the environment without unnecessary UI overlays, and find another solution before anxiety rises too high.

Open Room Ending
The final sequence of the game occurs in the Open Room.
- It acts as a conditional trigger that verifies if the Illusion, Loop, and Dependency room events have been completed.
- Upon completion, a final game-state transition is executed: the interaction collider is disabled, the anxiety level resets to zero, and the room maintains a calming blue lighting logic.

Art Style
The game uses a low-poly visual style optimized for WebGL performance.

Visual Direction
The aesthetic is designed to feel:
- symbolic
- dreamlike
- psychological
The environment is not intended to be realistic horror, but rather a visual representation of mental states.

Color Language
Color plays an important symbolic role.
Orange lighting: represents anxiety and tension.
Blue lighting: represents calmness and emotional balance.
As the player learns to manage anxiety, the house lighting gradually shifts from orange to blue.

UI / UX Design

Anxiety Meter
Displayed as a bar on the screen indicating the current anxiety level.

Intrusive Thought Visualization & Particle Effects
Mental states are represented by particle systems:
- Intrusive thoughts appear as particle effects partially blocking vision.
- Anxiety-driven environmental dust adds to the tense atmosphere.
- Relief-based effects provide visual feedback when the player calms down.

Guidance System
The game includes guidance by a text that:
- explains mechanics
- tells the player what to do
And a voice explanation about anxiety, helping the player understand the situation.

Audio Design

Music
The game includes background music that changes depending on anxiety level.
- calm music at low anxiety
- tense music at high anxiety

Sound Design
Environmental sounds contribute to tension:
- distant voices
- unsettling ambient noises
- breathing sounds
- heartbeat sounds

Voice Design
Voices heard in the game are understandable words, representing intrusive thoughts.
A voice explains facts about anxiety in order to learn.

Narrative Design
The player character is anonymous, representing the player rather than a specific individual. This allows the experience to feel universal.

Environmental Storytelling
The game includes written notes scattered around the house. These notes provide insight into:
- anxiety
- coping mechanisms
- personal reflections and memories
